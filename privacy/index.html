Privacy policy

What stays in your browser, what leaves, and how to stop it.

Applies to Watch Later Tidy (TidyWL) version 1.6.0 and later, on Chrome, Microsoft Edge and Firefox. The Edge build is the Chrome one, so wherever this page says Chrome it means Edge too. Published 2026-08-29, ahead of the 1.6.0 release; effective from the day 1.6.0 ships. Versions before 1.6.0 sent nothing about you or your install anywhere; that changed in 1.6.0, and this page exists because of it. Updated 2026-09-07 for version 2.0.0, which adds a paid tier and a Lineup that reads your watch history, again on 2026-09-12 for Firefox's licence permission and the checkout on the pricing page, on 2026-09-18 for version 2.1.0, whose usage report adds counts of a free install looking at Pro and of special announcements, on 2026-09-26 for version 2.2.0, whose usage report splits the videos you remove by whether the whole list was selected, on 2026-09-28 for version 2.3.0, whose usage report counts uses of the four Export & open buttons, on 2026-10-08 for version 2.5.0, whose usage report counts how often a free install met the free removal limit, on 2026-10-14 for version 2.6.0, whose uninstall link carries your own counts after a # that is never sent, and on 2026-10-21 for version 2.7.0, whose uninstall link adds the version, the install's random ID and a few more counts after that same #, which the page then sent nowhere, on 2026-10-22 for the same version, whose usage report also counts uses of four filter controls and of five Pro features, and on 2026-10-24 for the uninstall page, whose answer now goes with that random ID, the version and the counts, and with an email if you leave one for a reply, and on the same day for version 2.7.1, whose usage report adds the most videos you removed in a single run and the most YouTube lookups made in one hour, and on 2026-10-25 for the uninstall page, which now records an uninstall as soon as it opens, before any reason is picked. If you never buy Pro, the TidyWL Pro sections below do not apply to you.

The short version

  • Your YouTube data stays in your browser. Titles, video IDs, channels, your playlists, your watch history, your subscriptions — none of it is sent to us, ever. The one exception is Send to Gemini Notebook, which passes the links you picked to Google when you click the button. That is the whole point of that feature, and it is described below.
  • What does leave is a random identifier and some counts. After you delete, unlike or move videos, sync, build a Lineup, open the dashboard for the first time in a day, look at the Pro plans, see a special announcement, use one of the Export & open buttons, use a filter, or use a Pro feature, the extension sends tidywl.com a random ID for your install and your lifetime totals: videos deleted, likes removed, videos moved, bulk operations run, Lineups built, Lineups saved as a playlist, days you opened the dashboard, the size of your Watch Later, how often a free install looked at Pro and from where, special announcements shown and clicked, how many of the videos you removed went with the whole list selected and how many with part of it, how many times you used Send to Gemini Notebook, Export, Download transcripts and Open in Tabs, how many times you used the Filter menu, the Music filter, the Playlists menu's tabs and the sidebar's multi-select, how many times a Pro install ran a clean-up rule, pinned, restored from its delete history, applied a saved view and archived videos, and which browser you use. From version 2.7.1 it also sends three highest-ever numbers: the most videos you removed in a single run, kept apart for runs that used the removal pace and runs that did not, and the most YouTube lookups the extension made in any one clock hour. Never which videos, and never when.
  • There is no switch in the extension to turn this off on Chrome. Uninstalling stops it. On Firefox the switch is Firefox's own: the Technical and interaction data toggle under the add-on's Permissions and data.
  • Records not updated for 12 months are deleted.
  • The uninstall page sends that same random ID when it opens, and again with any answer you give. With it go the version, the store and the counts from the page's link, on Firefox only while that toggle is on. Opening it records that this install was removed; a reason you pick replaces the "no reason picked" mark. An email, if you leave one for a reply, is used once and deleted within 30 days.
  • If you buy Pro, your licence key goes to tidywl.com too. It switches Pro on and is checked again at most every 15 minutes while the dashboard is open, so a cancelled subscription or a device you removed elsewhere stops working here quickly. On Firefox it asks your permission first. Paddle takes the payment; your card details and the email you pay with never reach us. The one question on the page after buying is anonymous unless you type an email there for a reply, which is deleted after 28 days.

What stays in your browser

TidyWL works by calling YouTube's own endpoints with the session you are already signed in with, exactly as the YouTube web app does. Everything it fetches — your Watch Later, your Liked videos, any playlist you open, and the per-video details on each card — is cached in your browser's extension storage so the dashboard can search, sort and filter thousands of videos without re-fetching. That cache is namespaced per Google account and per playlist. It is never uploaded anywhere, and it goes away when you uninstall the extension.

The Lineup reads two more things, and they stay here as well. To suggest what to watch next, the Lineup reads your own YouTube watch history and your list of subscribed channels — the same way it reads a playlist, through YouTube's own endpoints with the session you are already signed in with. It also asks YouTube which category each video is in, so it can tell music from the rest. All three are cached in extension storage, per Google account, and re-read about once a day: wl_watch_history_<account>, wl_subscriptions_<account> and wl_video_category_<account>. The whole ranking — which videos are picked, and why — is worked out on your machine. None of it is sent to tidywl.com or anywhere else, and nothing derived from it appears in the usage report below. Uninstalling removes all three.

The free removal allowance is counted here too. On an install made from version 2.5.0 on, the free plan removes up to 1,000 hand-picked videos every 30 days, and the 30 days start with the first removal that counts. The day the current 30 days started and the count of what you have used since are kept as wl_removal_allowance, and a mark saying the install is new enough to have the limit as wl_removal_limited, in extension storage, once per install rather than per Google account. Neither is sent anywhere, and uninstalling removes both.

So is the day you installed it. An install made from version 2.7.0 on keeps the time it was installed as wl_installed_at, in extension storage, so the link opened when you remove TidyWL can say how many whole days you kept it (see The uninstall page below). It is not in the usage report, and only those whole days go out, with an answer on the uninstall page; it is never set on an install that only updated, and uninstalling removes it.

No sign-in, no account with us, no API key, no OAuth token. TidyWL cannot read your YouTube data when you are not signed in to YouTube in that browser. Pro does not change that: a licence key is not an account. There is no password, no profile, and we never learn your email address unless you type one in yourself to get a reply (see What you send yourself).

What TidyWL sends to tidywl.com

Three things, all to this site and nowhere else. The third only happens if you buy Pro.

1. A public configuration file

On each YouTube page load the extension fetches a small public JSON file (/config/api-config-v2.json) and, when you open the dashboard, a notices file (/config/announcements.json). These tell the extension where to find fields in YouTube's responses, so that many YouTube-side changes can be fixed without shipping a new version. The requests carry no identifier and no payload; everyone receives the same bytes. Nothing fetched is executed as code. From version 2.1.0 a notice can open once as a window when you open the dashboard, a special announcement: the extension draws it from that file as plain text and links, and only links to tidywl.com, our Discord, the browser stores, GitHub and Ko-fi are made clickable. From version 2.7.2 a special announcement can also show a picture. The extension loads it only from tidywl.com as the announcement opens; like those two requests it carries no identifier, and it does not say which page you were on.

2. A usage report

Each install carries a random identifier, generated in your browser when the extension is installed. It is not derived from, linked to, or stored alongside your Google account, your email, or anything else about you, and it is not shown anywhere in the extension. Reinstalling generates a new one.

Whenever one of your lifetime counts changes — after a delete, an unlike, a move, a sync, a Lineup, the first time you open the dashboard on a given day, a look at the Pro plans, or a special announcement — the extension sends that identifier and the counts to https://tidywl.com/api/installs. Never sent: a video title, a video ID, a channel, a playlist name, a URL, a timestamp of when you did anything, your watch progress, your watch history, your subscriptions, your Google account or email, your licence key, your extension settings, or an IP address the extension has read. The report format has no field that could hold any of these, so none can be sent by mistake.

The two Lineup counts are counts and nothing else. They say how often the feature was used, never what it suggested, what you watched, or which playlist you saved.

Days opened is a number, not a diary. The report carries how many days, never which days, what time or for how long. The server does note the date each report arrives; see How long it is kept, and where.

Looking at Pro is a count, not a sales lead. The fourteen counts from gate_lineup to gate_limit say how often a free install reached for Pro, and from where. Only a free install can move them. They are not linked to a licence key, a purchase or a checkout, so they cannot tell us who bought Pro, or whether anyone did.

Special announcements are counted, never named. The report says how many you were shown and how many you clicked, never which ones or what they said. Like every count here, a change is reported on the next YouTube page load, so looking at the Pro plans or seeing an announcement can add a report that day.

Whole list or part of it splits counts already sent. removed_whole and removed_partial divide the videos you remove by whether you had the whole list selected or only part of it. They never say which list, which videos or when, and they start at zero on the update to 2.2.0, so anything removed before it stays in the totals above and nowhere here.

The Export & open buttons are counted, never what went through them. notebook_sends, exports, transcript_runs and tab_opens go up by one each time one of those buttons does something: never by the number of videos, and never with which videos, which notebook, which file or which tabs. Nothing is counted when there were no videos, or when you answer Cancel to the question Open in Tabs or Download transcripts asks before a long run. They start at zero on the update to 2.3.0.

Filters are counted, never what they showed. multi_select, music_filter, playlist_tabs and filter_menu go up by one each time you press that control: never with which channels, topics, playlist or videos you picked or saw. They start at zero on the update to 2.7.0.

Pro features are counted, never what they touched. pro_rules, pro_pins, pro_restores, pro_views and pro_archives go up by one each time a press of that Pro feature did something: never by the number of videos, and never with which rule, video, view or playlist. Only an install with Pro can reach them, and nothing in the report says whether an install has Pro. They start at zero on the update to 2.7.0.

The biggest run and the busiest hour are highest-ever numbers, not totals. run_max_paced and run_max_unpaced hold the most videos you removed in a single run: one for runs that went at the steady pace very large removals get from version 2.6.0, one for runs that did not. player_hour_max holds the most YouTube lookups the extension made in any one clock hour. Each only ever rises, so a smaller run or a quieter hour changes nothing. None says which videos, which list, which day or which hour, and the report still carries no time. An install on an older version sends none of them, and they start at zero on the update to 2.7.1.

Counts include what you did before 1.6.0. The extension has counted deletions locally since version 1.4.1 (that is what powers the "you've cleaned up N videos" note). Those totals are reported on the first YouTube page load after updating, so a long-time user's first report already carries their history. One consequence: likes removed before 1.6.0 were counted as deletions at the time and stay in the deleted total; they cannot be separated out after the fact.

Opening the dashboard sends at most one report a day. The first time you open it on a given day, open_days goes up by one and that change is reported; opening it again the same day sends nothing. This is what lets us count how many people use TidyWL in a week, not only how many clean something up. There is still no timer and no install beacon: a report is sent only when a count has changed, an install that has never opened the dashboard or done anything counted above sends nothing at all, and an install that is idle sends nothing while it is idle.

3. A licence check, if you buy Pro

A free install never calls these endpoints. Nothing in this section happens until you enter a key.

Buying Pro gets you a licence key — about twenty random characters, made by our server. It is not derived from your email, your name or anything else about you. It is kept in the extension's synced storage, so it follows your browser profile to your other computers.

The extension sends that key to https://tidywl.com/api/license/… to switch Pro on, and sends it again to check the subscription is still live — at most once every 15 minutes, and only while the dashboard is open. The interval is short on purpose: it is how a cancelled subscription, or a device you removed from another computer, stops working here within minutes instead of a day. If the server cannot be reached at all, Pro keeps working for 14 days and then switches itself off.

Each call carries the key, the activation ID for this device, and — the first time only — the device name you typed. Nothing about your playlists, your videos, your watch history or your Google account goes with it. The same is true of the button that removes a device and the one that opens the billing portal.

On Firefox, none of these calls happens until you allow it. Firefox files a licence check under Authentication information, an optional permission that TidyWL declares and does not assume. The first time you press Activate, remove a device or open Manage subscription without it, TidyWL opens a page explaining the check and Firefox shows its own prompt. Say no and nothing is sent. While that prompt is open, the call you started waits in the browser's memory, never on disk, so it can finish by itself when you allow it. Without the permission the automatic check sends nothing either, and Pro pauses 14 days after the last successful check.

Why

To know how heavily TidyWL is actually used — how many people open it in a week, how many installs clean up in one big purge versus a little every week, how large the lists are — which decides what gets built next. Reviews and donations are the only other signal and both come from a self-selected few. The numbers are used for that and for a public total on this site. They are not sold, not shared with anyone, and not used for advertising.

How to stop it

  • Chrome (and other Chromium browsers): there is no setting in the extension to turn reporting off. Uninstalling the extension stops it, and the record expires after 12 months of no updates. We chose not to add a switch rather than add one and then design around people flipping it; that trade-off is ours and this page says so plainly.
  • Firefox: Firefox shows a Technical and interaction data toggle for this add-on. On a fresh install it is in the install prompt, pre-ticked, and you can change it any time afterwards under about:addons → TidyWL → Permissions and data. If you already had TidyWL and updated to 1.6.0, Firefox does not ask and the toggle starts off — so the dashboard asks you once, the first time you open it after the update, and either answer can be changed there later. When it is off, the extension sends no report.
  • The licence check is different, and on Chrome there is no switch for it. It is how Pro works: if the key stopped being checked, there would be nothing keeping a cancelled subscription from running forever. Deactivating this device in the About modal removes the key, stops the calls, and puts the extension back to free. On Firefox the switch is Firefox's own Authentication information permission, in the same place as the toggle above: turn it off and no check is sent, and Pro pauses 14 days after the last one.

How long it is kept, and where

One record per install, on a server we run at tidywl.com — a Cloudflare Worker with a Cloudflare D1 database. The server keeps the highest value it has seen for each count and the latest Watch Later size. It also keeps three dates, the day only and never a time: the day your install's first report arrived, the day its latest one did, and the latest day one of your delete, unlike, move or Lineup counts went up. Because the first dashboard open of a day sends a report, the second date is in practice the last day you opened the dashboard. Once a day the server also adds every install's figures up into daily totals — how many installs reported in the last week, how many were new, how many did a clean-up — kept in tables that name no install, and so kept after the records they were added up from expire. A record that has not been updated for 12 months is deleted; before deletion its counts are added to a single running total so the public number never goes backwards, and after deletion nothing about that install remains.

The server does not keep request logs, does not read or store the IP address your report arrives from, and has no error-tracking service attached. Cloudflare, which fronts the site, rate-limits the endpoint per address at its edge; that happens in Cloudflare's infrastructure under Cloudflare's own policies, and nothing about the address reaches the server or its database.

TidyWL Pro — what the licence record holds

Buying Pro creates rows in that same database. There are two kinds, and this is all of it.

The licence

One row per subscription. It holds a fingerprint of your key (the key itself is stored encrypted, so the checkout page can show it to you again, and never in readable form), which plan you are on, whether the subscription is active, past due, cancelled, expired or refunded, whether it is a trial, how many devices it allows, when the next charge is due, when the paid period ends, and Paddle's own identifiers for the subscription and for you as their customer.

Not your email, not your name, not your address, not one digit of your card. None of those ever reach us from Paddle. An email you choose to type with the question on the page after buying is kept with that answer, never on this row, and is deleted after 28 days. Paddle's customer identifier is the only thing on the row that points at a person, and only Paddle can turn it back into one.

The activation

One row per device you switch Pro on. It holds an activation ID we generate, which licence it belongs to, the device name, when it was activated, and when it last checked in. The name is yours to choose — the dashboard fills the box with something like "Chrome on Mac" so the list is not full of blanks, and you can type anything over it.

A key activates on 3 devices. The About modal shows the whole list — every device, its name, when it was added, when it was last seen — and you can remove any of them from any device. Removing one frees the seat straight away; that device drops back to free at its next check, within 15 minutes.

Deactivating deletes the activation row outright. The licence row stays while the subscription exists, and after you cancel, so the key still works if you come back. Ask us and we will delete that too — see below.

Renewal dates

When Paddle tells our server that a subscription started, renewed, failed or was cancelled, the message carries the date of the next charge and the date the paid period ends. Both are stored on the licence row, and the extension reads them back. That is the only reason the About modal can say "Renews Oct 5, 2026" instead of saying nothing.

Paddle, who takes the money

TidyWL Pro is sold through Paddle, which is the merchant of record — legally the seller, and the one that charges you, files the sales tax and handles refunds. The checkout is Paddle's own form, opened over the pricing page on this site in an ordinary browser tab, never inside the extension.

Your card details, billing address and email go to Paddle, and never to us. What Paddle tells us is: this subscription exists, this is its plan, this is whether it is paid, and here are Paddle's own reference numbers for the subscription, for the customer and for each payment. What Paddle does with what you gave them is covered by Paddle's privacy policy, not this one.

The page you land on after paying. When the checkout finishes, the pricing page sends you to a page on this site with the payment's transaction number in the address, and that page trades the number for your key. The address carries nothing else.

Finding a lost key. Every Paddle receipt shows that transaction number. Paste it into the same page, or into Have a key? in the extension, and it goes to our server once, to look your key up. The lookup stores nothing new: the number is already on your licence, recorded when Paddle told us about the payment. The same number is enough to see the key, so treat your receipts the way you would the key.

Manage subscription. The button in the About modal asks our server for a link into Paddle's customer portal, where you can cancel, change your card or download invoices. The server looks up your Paddle customer identifier, asks Paddle for a signed-in link to that account, and hands it straight back for the browser to open in a new tab. It only happens when you click it. That link is a credential, so it is never written down: not stored, not logged, not kept once the tab is open.

Requests about your data

Because the usage identifier is random and lives only in your browser's extension storage, we cannot tell which usage record is yours, and neither can you without reading the extension's storage directly. There is therefore no lookup, export or delete-on-request for those records; expiry after 12 months of inactivity is the deletion mechanism. The one exception is an email you leave on the uninstall page: while it is kept, it sits next to that answer's install ID, and if you write from that address we will delete it sooner. If you have a question anyway, write to support@tidywl.com.

A Pro licence is different, because the key identifies it. Write to support@tidywl.com from any address and quote your key, and we can tell you exactly what is on your licence row, or delete the row and every activation on it. Deleting it is permanent — the key stops working and cannot be restored — so cancel your subscription with Paddle first. Anything Paddle holds, including your email and your invoices, has to be asked of Paddle.

What you send yourself

Send to Gemini Notebook takes the videos you selected and adds their links as sources to a Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM), using your own Google session. Those links go to Google, because that is the entire point of the feature. It only happens when you click the button, and only after you have granted the browser permission for notebook.google.com that the extension asks for the first time you use it. Google's handling of what arrives there is covered by Google's privacy policy, not this one.

Request a refund, in the About card, copies a message to our support address with your licence key in it and opens Gmail's compose window with that message already written. The key is in the link to Gmail from the moment you click, so it reaches Google, in your own mailbox, whether or not you send it; it reaches us only when you do. It only happens when you click it.

The uninstall page. When you remove TidyWL, your browser opens tidywl.com/uninstall/, which asks why. Answering is up to you, but opening the page is not silent: since 2026-10-25, whenever the link holds the install's random ID, the page sends the details below as soon as it opens, marked no reason picked, even if you click nothing. Clicking a reason sends us that reason, which replaces the mark; if you then type something and press Send note, it sends what you typed too, up to 2,000 characters. Both go with a random ID the page draws when it opens, so changing your answer replaces it rather than adding a second, and one install leaves one row however many times the page is opened. Since 2026-10-24 an answer also carries what the page's link holds (next paragraph), whenever the link holds the install's random ID: that ID, the extension's version, the store, the counts of videos removed and moved, how many whole days ago it was installed, and whether it had the free removal limit. It is the same ID the usage report sends, so an answer can be read next to that install's counts. The ID is random, made by the extension, and is not tied to your name, email or licence. Never a title, a video, a channel or anything about your Google account. On Firefox the link leaves the ID out while Firefox's Technical and interaction data setting is off, and then the page sends none of this, with or without an answer. Under a reason about something going wrong (it never opened, it stopped working, it was too slow, or something else), you can also leave an email if you want a reply. It is optional, used once to answer you, and deleted within 30 days, and changing your answer without it removes it; while it is kept, it sits next to that answer and its ID. The server keeps the reason, what you typed, the details above and the day, never a time, and deletes them 12 months later. To stop one address flooding the form, the server hands the address an answer arrives from to Cloudflare's rate limiter, and nowhere else: it is not stored or logged. Leave anything else that identifies you out of the note; if you would rather not leave an email, write to support@tidywl.com for an answer.

Your own count, in that link. From version 2.6.0, the link your browser opens carries this install's own totals after a #: how many videos it removed or unliked, how many it moved to another playlist, and which store it came from. From version 2.7.0 it also carries the extension's version, a mark when the install has the free removal allowance, how many whole days ago it was installed (only on an install made from 2.7.0 on, which is when that day started being kept), and the install's random ID, the same one the usage report already sends. On Firefox the ID is left out while Firefox's Technical and interaction data setting is off. For example tidywl.com/uninstall/#c=1222&m=56&s=chrome&v=2.7.0&l=1&a=40&i=…. That is all it can carry: counts, the version, the mark and the random ID, never a title, a video ID, a channel or anything about your Google account. Browsers never send the part after a # to a server; the page reads it in your browser. It uses the counts and the store to show you your count if you answer I finished my cleanup and to pick the store's rating button, and it sends all of it with your answer, and since 2026-10-25 as soon as the page opens, as above, but only when it holds the install's random ID. A value that does not look exactly as the extension writes it is dropped, not sent. The link does stay in your browser history, like any page you visit.

Permissions, and what each one is for

  • youtube.com — where the extension works. Reading, reordering and deleting your playlists is done by calling YouTube with your existing session, and so is the Lineup's read of your watch history and your subscriptions.
  • tidywl.com — the config and notices files, the usage report, and, for Pro, the licence check. All described above.
  • Storage and unlimited storage — the local playlist caches. "Unlimited" only raises the local quota so that syncing every playlist you own does not fail part-way; it sends nothing.
  • Active tab — so the toolbar popup can tell whether you are on a playlist page.
  • Scripting — used for one thing: adding sources to a Gemini Notebook tab, after you have granted that site.
  • Downloads (asked for the first time you download transcripts) — writing transcript files to your Downloads folder. Decline it and the extension offers a Save button instead.
  • notebook.google.com (asked the first time you send to Gemini Notebook) — see above. Decline it and the links are copied to your clipboard instead.
  • Authentication information (Firefox only, asked the first time you activate Pro) — the licence check described above. Decline it and nothing is sent, and Pro cannot be switched on in that browser.

Four questions you can answer, and two counts. Since 2026-10-22 three pages talk to https://tidywl.com/api/feedback, and the questions send only when you tap an answer or press Send:

  • The page you land on after buying Pro asks What made you buy today?, below the steps that switch Pro on, with four answers, a box for a note and, if you want a reply, a box for an email.
  • The feedback page, tidywl.com/feedback/, which the dashboard, a link in a store reply, the Pro card and the free-limit dialog open. It asks a Pro subscriber how they would feel if Pro went away and what they use it for most. It asks anyone else which feature they would miss most if Watch Later Tidy disappeared tomorrow, a tap with an optional note, and, from the Pro card or the limit dialog, what has kept them from getting Pro, a tap and nothing more.
  • The uninstall page and the feedback page each count every time they open. A count sends the name of the page and nothing else, so the answers people give can be read against how many saw the question. Nothing from the uninstall link's # goes with its count; the separate record described under The uninstall page is sent only when the link holds the install's random ID.

An answer carries the answer you tapped, what you typed (up to 2,000 characters), whether you are on Free or Pro, which link brought you to the page (the dashboard, a store reply, the Pro card or the limit dialog), the extension's version when the dashboard put it in the link, and a random id for this one answer, so an edited answer replaces itself rather than adding a second; it is not linked to your install. The server keeps those and the day, never a time. It does not store your IP address, your licence key, your install's random ID or anything about your YouTube account, and nothing in an answer names your purchase. Answers are anonymous, with one exception: on the page after buying Pro, you can type an email if you would like a reply. It goes only with a note, is used only to reply to you, and is deleted 28 days later; sending your answer again without it removes it, and while it is kept it sits next to that answer. The page never reads or sends the email you paid with; only an address you type yourself goes. Everywhere else these forms cannot reply, so leave out anything that identifies you.

Visiting this website

Everything above is about the extension. This section is about tidywl.com itself, which is a separate thing you may use without ever installing anything.

This site counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, and nothing else. Since 2026-09-15 each page loads a small script from Cloudflare, static.cloudflareinsights.com/beacon.min.js, which reports back to tidywl.com/cdn-cgi/rum: the page you opened, the site that linked you here, your country, your browser, operating system and device type, and how long the page took to load. Cloudflare says it uses no cookie or local storage to track visitors, does not fingerprint them by IP address or browser, and does not follow them across other sites. We see totals, such as how many visits a page had and which sites sent them, never a person. Cloudflare keeps the full detail for 7 days and a sample of about a tenth after that. There is no Google Analytics, no Plausible, no Fathom, no pixel, no tag manager and no session recorder, and nothing here records what you click. An ad blocker or Brave stops the script, and the site works the same without it.

We set no cookies of our own. The site stores exactly one thing in your browser: whether you chose light or dark mode, kept in local storage under theme, written only when you click the toggle and never sent anywhere. Clear your site data and it is gone.

What that script does in your browser is covered by Paddle's privacy policy, not this one. No other page on this site loads anything from Paddle. No payment script runs here. Pro is not on sale yet, so no page on this site loads anything from Paddle. When it goes on sale, the pricing page will load Paddle's checkout script, Paddle.js, and no other page will.

Our payment provider sets one, on the pricing page only. That page loads Paddle's checkout script, and Paddle sets a short-lived cookie (__cf_bm, on paddle.com) to tell browsers apart from bots. It is a security measure, it expires within half an hour, and it is not used to profile you or to advertise. The rest of the site sets no cookie when it loads (checked 2026-10-22).

The demo video does not load until you press play. The thumbnail on the front page is an image on our own server, not an embed. Only when you click it does a YouTube player load, from youtube-nocookie.com, and only then does YouTube see you. If you never press play, no request reaches Google at all.

Fonts are served from here, not from Google. The typeface is stored on our own server, so loading a page on this site does not tell Google you did.

Web servers necessarily see the IP address of whoever asks for a page; Cloudflare's are kept briefly for security and abuse handling, and we do not build them into logs, profiles or reports of our own.

What is not in this policy, on purpose

No analytics library, no crash reporter, no advertising SDK, no third-party script of any kind runs inside the extension. The extension ships unminified — every file in the package is the source — so anyone who wants to check what it sends can read it rather than take this page's word for it.
